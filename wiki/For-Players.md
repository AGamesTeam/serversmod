# For Players: What's In It For You

ServerMod gives you a persistent economic life on the server, not just a survival playthrough — a balance that follows you everywhere, a business you can grow (or ignore and just work a market/trading angle), land you can defend with real permissions, and a government you can join, serve in, or found yourself. This page is a practical guide to getting value out of it, cross-linked to the detailed mechanics pages.

## The fastest ways to make money

- **Sell what you find, instantly.** Right-click a [Trader Block](Economy-Market.md#trader-block) holding anything the market tracks and your whole stack sells at the current price on the spot — no waiting, no listing, no travel. Good for turning raw drops (ores, crops, mob loot) into cash the moment you get them.
- **Automate it.** Point a hopper into an [Auto-Seller](Economy-Market.md#auto-seller) and any farm, mob grinder, or mining operation you own starts paying you passively, even while you're offline doing something else.
- **Watch the market, don't fight it.** Prices move against you as you trade — selling a lot at once pushes the price down, and it only recovers gradually. If you're selling in bulk, consider spreading large sales out over time, or across other players' businesses, rather than dumping everything on one Trader Block at once.
- **Trade directly.** `/trade money` and `/trade item` work even if the other player's offline — useful for player-run deals that don't need a business's overhead.

## Running a business is where the real money is

A registered [business](Businesses.md) turns you from a trader into a shopkeeper with a real storefront, real customers, and compounding advantages:

- **Reviews snowball.** A business trending toward great reviews gets both more frequent customers *and* customers who spend more per visit — up to 60% more on both counts. A few bad reviews do the opposite. This means early service quality (fair prices, keeping barrels stocked, not leaving customers waiting) pays off disproportionately later. See [Customer NPCs & Reviews](Customer-NPCs-Reviews.md#reviews--traffic-feedback-loop).
- **Upgrades are cheap insurance on your rating.** An Awning or Price Board costs a modest one-time fee and permanently softens how harshly customers judge you on price and general experience — worth buying early, before your review history is established.
- **Hire an employee.** For a small weekly wage, an Employee NPC auto-serves every waiting customer without you lifting a finger — meaning your shop keeps earning while you're off doing literally anything else, including offline.
- **A Business Sign is free and permanent.** Grab yours on registration (or re-claim it any time for free) and place it — it's the simplest way to make your shop discoverable to customer NPCs, and unlike an advert, it never expires.
- **Manufacturing turns cheap inputs into pricier outputs.** Check what recipes are available and whether the math works out better than just selling raw materials at the Trader Block.
- **Loyalty Card rewards patience.** Every 5th sale gets an extra-forgiving review — a slow trickle of reputation insurance that costs nothing per-sale after the initial purchase.

## Land: claim it before someone else does

- **Claim early, claim generously — within reason.** Claim size is capped per shovel use, but there's no cap on your total land, and shovel tiers are cheap relative to what they protect. An unclaimed base is fully unprotected; a claimed one blocks building, container access, and PvP by default.
- **Use trust deliberately.** Trust is per-player, per-permission — you can let a friend open your chests without letting them build, or vice versa. Don't blanket-trust when a narrower grant would do.
- **Watch your rent.** Land claims, cities, and countries are all billed weekly. Missing a payment can suspend or even dissolve what you own depending on the local policy — check `/land status` regularly if money's tight.
- **A public square puts your business on the map for free** if a nearby government has one — worth locating your storefront near one instead of purely for the view.

## Government: join it, serve in it, or start your own

- **Founding a city or country is a real commitment**, not a decoration — it immediately starts owing rent, and you (or whoever you appoint) have to actively manage rates, laws, and jails or it'll drift into suspension.
- **Officialdom is granular.** If you're offered a role in someone else's government, check exactly which permissions come with it (`manage_rates`, `manage_jails`, `manage_officials`, etc.) — you might have real teeth, or just a title.
- **Alliances pay off directly**, once mutual — allied governments' businesses avoid double taxation on shared sales, and their citizens shop at a 10% discount at your allied territory's businesses. Sanctions do the opposite and are much easier to fall into unilaterally, so know your neighbors' relationships before setting up shop across a border.
- **Report abuse instead of retaliating.** If someone violates a `reportable` rule on your claim, file a report instead of starting a fight — a resolved report can net you a real cut of the fine.

## Staying safe

- **Danger costs you reviews, if you're the one running the shop others visit.** Customer NPCs remember taking damage or seeing hostiles en route, and it shows up in what they write. Keep the paths to your storefront reasonably safe.
- **Theft is real if enabled and your barrels aren't protected by your own land claim** — a Guard Dog upgrade cuts that risk by 80%, and being in a "trade war" (a sanctioned relationship) triples it, so know your government's diplomatic standing.
- **Clothing laws are enforced every minute, not once.** If a country you're standing in requires a chestplate and leggings, put them on — you'll be fined repeatedly, not just once, for as long as you're caught out.
